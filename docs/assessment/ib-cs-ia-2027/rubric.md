# IB Computer Science IA 2027 Rubric Summary

This file is a paraphrased rubric summary for IA Supervisor AI review. It is intended to guide feedback generation, not to replace official assessment materials or teacher judgement.

## Review Principles

- Judge each criterion independently, but check consistency across connected criteria.
- Criterion A drives success criteria used by Criteria B, D, and E.
- Criterion B should translate the problem and success criteria into a plan.
- Criterion C should translate the plan into a reproducible system model, algorithms, and testing strategy.
- Criterion D should demonstrate a functioning coded product, justified implementation choices, and effective testing.
- Criterion E should evaluate the finished product against the success criteria.
- Do not reward unsupported claims. Feedback should refer to evidence found in the submitted work.
- Do not assign a final mark unless the teacher explicitly asks for a provisional estimate.

## Criterion A: Problem Specification, 4 marks

### Low evidence range: 1-2

The work gives a basic problem scenario, includes limited success criteria, and gives a simple outline of the computational solution. The problem may be too broad, weakly measurable, or only loosely connected to a computing context.

### High evidence range: 3-4

The work describes the problem in terms of measurable solution requirements, gives appropriate success criteria, and explains why the chosen computational context is suitable.

### Common AI Feedback Checks

- Are the success criteria measurable?
- Are the requirements specific enough to drive design and evaluation?
- Is the computing context explicit?
- Is the problem scenario connected to a real need, issue, user, or domain?

## Criterion B: Planning, 4 marks

### Low evidence range: 1-2

The work gives only a partial decomposition and a plan that addresses some, but not all, success criteria.

### High evidence range: 3-4

The work gives a reasonable decomposition into essential components and a plan that addresses the success criteria.

### Common AI Feedback Checks

- Is the decomposition complete enough to support implementation?
- Does the plan cover design, development, testing, and evaluation?
- Are Criterion A success criteria carried forward?
- Are tools, libraries, or research needs identified where relevant?

## Criterion C: System Overview, 6 marks

### Low evidence range: 1-2

The work outlines a limited system model, identifies some algorithms, and includes a testing strategy for at least one success criterion.

### Middle evidence range: 3-4

The work constructs an incomplete system model, gives algorithms that support partial functionality, and outlines a testing strategy aligned with at least three success criteria.

### High evidence range: 5-6

The work constructs a complete system model, gives algorithms that enable the product to perform, and describes a testing strategy aligned with the success criteria.

### Common AI Feedback Checks

- Are the key components and their relationships visible?
- Is the UI included where relevant?
- Could a third party understand how to recreate the product?
- Are algorithms tied to product components?
- Does the testing strategy cover the success criteria, including invalid or unexpected inputs?

## Criterion D: Development, 12 marks

### Low evidence range: 1-3

The product has very limited functionality. Techniques are absent or not appropriate for implementing the algorithms. Implementation choices and testing strategy are only stated.

### Basic evidence range: 4-6

The product has limited functionality. At least one appropriate technique is used. Implementation choices are outlined and the effectiveness of testing is stated.

### Strong evidence range: 7-9

The product has partial functionality. Some appropriate techniques are used. Implementation choices are explained and the effectiveness of testing is described.

### High evidence range: 10-12

The product is fully functional. Appropriate techniques are used to implement the algorithms. Implementation choices are evaluated and the effectiveness of the testing strategy is justified.

### Common AI Feedback Checks

- Is there evidence that the product actually works?
- Are implementation techniques appropriate for the algorithms and system design?
- Does the student explain why important implementation choices were made?
- Does the student evaluate the choices rather than only describe them?
- Are tests linked to correctness, reliability, and efficiency?
- Are examples from testing connected to evidence in the video?
- Are code excerpts readable, relevant, and connected to the appendix?

## Criterion E: Evaluation, 4 marks

### Low evidence range: 1-2

The work states how far the success criteria were met and describes possible improvements.

### High evidence range: 3-4

The work evaluates how far the success criteria were met and justifies improvements.

### Common AI Feedback Checks

- Does the evaluation explicitly revisit the success criteria from Criterion A?
- Are conclusions supported by testing, product evidence, or user/client feedback?
- Are improvements specific and justified?
- Does the student explain why each improvement would improve the product?

## Cross-Criterion Consistency Checks

- Criterion A success criteria should appear again in B planning, C testing strategy, D testing evidence, and E evaluation.
- Criterion C algorithms should be visible in Criterion D implementation.
- Criterion D testing should match the strategy proposed in Criterion C.
- Criterion E should evaluate the actual product, not a different or idealized product.
- The video should support Criterion D functionality and testing claims.
- The appendix should contain full source code for referenced code excerpts.
